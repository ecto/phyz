What it is

phyz is a differentiable simulation engine for articulated rigid bodies, particles, electromagnetic fields, molecular dynamics, and beyond. Every algorithm computes gradients for optimization, control, and learning.

Plug in semi-implicit Euler, RK4, or your own solver. Load MuJoCo MJCF models. Fit simulation parameters to real trajectories. Batch thousands of environments on the GPU.

The numerics come from tang: generic scalars for dual numbers and exact derivatives, dense linear algebra, and a tensor IR that lowers to CUDA, Metal, Vulkan, and WGSL. phyz is the physics on top.

phyz is early, ambitious, and open source.

Eighteen demos, running in this tab

Every card is a phyz crate compiled to WASM and drawn live, with the simulation's own numbers under it. Each one says what is happening and what you would use it for.

01 · Bodies

Articulated rigid bodies on Featherstone's spatial algebra: O(n) forward dynamics, contacts through GJK/EPA, energy you can audit. The part of phyz a robot is made of.

02 · Particles

Discrete elements, the material point method, and mass-spring bodies. For the materials that don't have a shape until you simulate them: grains, soils, jellies.

03 · Fields

Grids instead of bodies: Maxwell on a Yee lattice, Boltzmann on D2Q9, and a thousand pendulums drawn as one surface. Waves, flows, and sweeps.

04 · Robots

Articulated trees under control. Torques in, the same O(n) dynamics out, one body or a hundred at once. This is what the controller runs against.

05 · Gradients

Every simulation on this page is differentiable. These three put the gradient to work, and two of them differentiate through contact, which is where most engines stop.

06 · Soft

Position-based dynamics with real compliance, meters per newton, so the stretch you see is a material property and not an iteration count. Cloth, cables, and anything that hangs.
